Chapter 01

Discover Australia's Shorebirds

Australia provides important habitats for many shorebird species. Explore which species appear most frequently in the records and how observations vary across seasons and years.

Visualisation 01

Meet Australia's Shorebirds

Discover the most frequently listed shorebird species.

Key observation: The Sharp-tailed Sandpiper is the most frequently listed species, appearing in 141 records. This reflects its frequency in the dataset, rather than its total population in Australia.

Visualisation 02

Shorebird Records by Season

Compare the number of records in summer and winter.

Key observation: Summer accounts for the majority of shorebird records, with 1,269 observations. This suggests that more records were collected during summer than winter in this dataset.

Visualisation 03

Shorebird Species by Season

Explore how species records differ between seasons.

Key observation: Most of the displayed shorebird species have more records in summer. However, the Double-banded Plover shows the opposite pattern, with more records during winter.

Visualisation 04

Shorebird Records Over Time

Examine changes in recorded shorebird observations across the available years.

Key observation: Shorebird records fluctuate between 2010 and 2024, with noticeable peaks in 2010 and 2016. Records decline towards 2024, although this does not necessarily indicate a decline in shorebird populations.

From sightings to habitats: Understanding shorebird records is only part of the story. The next section explores the places where shorebirds gather and how species diversity relates to reported bird counts.
Chapter 02

Exploring Shorebird Habitats

Shorebirds depend on suitable habitats for feeding, resting and gathering. Explore which areas have reported the largest peak counts and whether areas with more recorded species also have higher counts.

Visualisation 05

Australia's Key Shorebird Gathering Areas

Compare the highest reported peak bird counts across shorebird areas.

Key observation: Eighty Mile Beach has the highest reported peak count at 520,000 shorebirds, nearly four times that of Roebuck Bay (135,725). This highlights the importance of major shorebird gathering areas.

Visualisation 06

Do More Diverse Areas Have Higher Bird Counts?

Explore the relationship between species richness and reported peak counts.

Key observation: Areas with more recorded shorebird species generally show higher peak counts, although the relationship varies. One area exceeds 500,000 birds, highlighting an exceptionally large reported gathering.

Why wetlands matter: Wetlands provide important feeding and resting environments for shorebirds. The final chapter explores Australia's internationally recognised Ramsar wetlands and their geographic distribution.
Chapter 03

Australia's Important Wetlands

Ramsar wetlands are internationally recognised for their ecological importance. Explore how these sites are distributed across Australian states and territories, where individual sites are located, and which regions show greater geographic concentration.

Visualisation 07

Ramsar Wetlands by State

Compare the number of Ramsar sites across Australian states and territories.

Key observation: New South Wales has the highest number of Ramsar wetlands with 13 sites, followed closely by Victoria and Western Australia with 12 each. This shows that internationally recognised wetlands are distributed across several Australian regions.

Visualisation 08

Mapping Australia's Important Wetlands

Explore the state-level distribution of Ramsar wetland sites across Australia.

Key observation: New South Wales has the highest number of Ramsar sites (13), followed by Victoria and Western Australia (12 each). Darker green regions represent states with more internationally recognised wetlands.

Visualisation 09

Where Are Australia's Ramsar Wetlands Located?

Hover over the map markers to discover individual Ramsar wetland locations.

Key observation: Ramsar wetlands are distributed across Australia, with many sites located near coastal regions. Each dot represents one Ramsar site.

Visualisation 10

Where Are Ramsar Wetlands Most Concentrated?

Explore how Ramsar wetland sites are distributed across Australia using geographic grid cells. Darker blue cells represent higher site counts.

Key observation: Ramsar sites appear more concentrated in parts of southeastern Australia. Darker blue cells represent more sites within each 3-degree geographic grid cell. These cells show geographic groupings, not the boundaries of individual wetlands.
